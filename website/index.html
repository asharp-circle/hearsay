Hearsay is local speech-to-text for macOS. Release the key and your words land right where your cursor is — transcribed on your Mac, by your Mac. No cloud, no account, works on a plane.

Screenshot the full screen mid-recording⌥ 3

Screenshots taken while you talk get referenced in the transcript — say "this button here" and the image rides along. Shortcuts are remappable in Settings.

Why people keep it running

Small tool, sharp edges filed off

Nothing leaves your Mac

Audio is transcribed by a model running on your machine. Quit your network and it still works. There's no account to make.

Pasted, not narrated

Text lands at your cursor and on the clipboard — into your editor, a chat box, a commit message, wherever you were typing.

History that forgives

Every transcription is saved locally. If one fails, the audio is kept — retry it from History with one click instead of saying it all again.

Cleanup rules

Optional filters tidy the raw transcript — filler words out, your custom replacements in — before it's pasted.

Menu bar resident

Lives in the menu bar with your recent transcriptions one click away. The full window is there when you want to dig.

Open source

MIT licensed. Read it, fork it, wire it into your own tools.

For developers

It's also a dictation provider

Hearsay ships a CLI, and other apps can use it as a local dictation backend — editors, launchers, your own scripts. See the integration guide.

# dictate from a script, print the transcript
hearsay dictate

# everything you've said lately
hearsay history

# when something's off
hearsay logs
